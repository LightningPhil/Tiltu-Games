# The arcade collection

Tiltu’s Games contains 54 games. TetriGrid, Archive Patrol, Neon Void, Whack-a-Party!, Neon Horizon and Neon Synth Ballistics live in **Arcade & worlds**, share the collection’s five themes and navigation, and work in the offline HTML. Their playfields fit the viewport; longer setup panels scroll independently. Soundtracks are synthesized locally. No network services or runtime downloads are needed.

## Four standalone game ports

`src/arcade-ports/` contains collection-owned copies of the gameplay, rendering, audio, settings and view modules from `apps/neon-void`, `apps/whack-a`, `apps/neon-horozon` and `apps/neon-synth-ballistics`. Those original apps are retained unchanged. There are no iframes, standalone HTML loaders or runtime imports from another app. `scripts/import-arcade-sources.mjs` records the one-time source extraction; subsequent adaptations are maintained directly in the collection modules, so rerunning that script would overwrite them.

- **Neon Void:** thirty-wave, three-act expedition, six commanders and final fleet; endless continuation; checkpoint resupply, repair and fitting economy; three unlockable ships, nine weapon mounts, ordnance and system upgrades; career records; any-wave practice; pointer/touch flight; nine synthesized tracks and the ending/victory scores.
- **Whack-a-Party!:** eight illustrated animals and mixed zoo; locally prepared custom PNG characters and gallery; three paces; timed/practice rounds; protected sleepy targets, hats, crowns, combos and fever; configurable tap allowances and miss delay; character-specific sound, records and accessible keyboard/touch controls.
- **Neon Horizon:** procedural 3D city, seeded routes, flight school, sector interception, endless hunt and peaceful cruise; three difficulty settings; mouse stick, relative mouse, keyboard, gamepad and touch input; full flight attitude, collision, combat and effects; detailed/retro visuals, three cameras, comfort settings and eight synthesized tracks. Three.js 0.128.0 is bundled locally, with its MIT notice retained in the source HTML and offline build.
- **Neon Synth Ballistics:** 3-vs-3 rovers, destructible terrain, ten weapons/tools, wind, retreat and parachutes; three CPU settings, shared-device play and unlimited firing range; map codes and recent maps; squad/unit names, turn lengths and sudden death; camera/minimap controls; three synthesized tracks and touch controls.

`PortScope` owns each game's DOM queries, global input listeners, animation frames, timers, resize observation and audio contexts. Leaving pauses its engine, releases input/pointer lock and suspends audio. Returning reuses the current view and engine in a paused state. Changing the shared theme preserves the same engine. Launch controls are moved into compact, immediately accessible action groups; setup and records remain in explicit scrolling panels. Imported CSS selectors and animation names are scoped, type and panel colours use collection tokens, and original game artwork stays intact.

Settings, records, Neon Void dock checkpoints and custom characters live inside separate `actionArcade` entries in the collection's existing `grid-club-v1` save. Standalone storage namespaces are never read or overwritten. Live engines survive switching games during the visit; a browser reload restores the games' own persistent records/settings/checkpoints, rather than promising a mid-flight or mid-shot save. Storage failures roll back rejected writes and leave the collection usable during the visit.

Copied engine regressions are under `tests/ports-*.test.ts`, including deterministic campaign/economy simulations, flight and collision tests, terrain/weapon tests, audio scheduling, character validation and storage. `tests/ports-integration.test.ts` checks save isolation, quota rollback and lifecycle ownership. On isolated port **5193**, `/tests/ports-playground.html` exercises 300 menu/play/pause layouts across all five themes and five screen sizes, plus live controls, theme changes, pause/resume and navigation. Add `?bundle` for the offline build. Fixtures are development-only and excluded from the deliverable. `tests/results/ports-standalone-check.json` records the original-folder hash comparison.

## TetriGrid

The previous Polyrhythm Blocks experiment has been replaced with a port of this repository’s **standalone TetraGrid** in `apps/tetra-grid`, at the user’s request. The standalone folder is retained unchanged. The collection owns its own copy of the engine, music, instruments and audio transport in `src/tetrigrid`; there is no iframe, runtime dependency on the other app, or standalone UI embedded in the collection.

The port preserves:

- Seven tetrominoes, shuffled seven-bag, 10 × 20 visible field plus two hidden spawn rows, five previews, landing outline, once-per-piece hold, separate SRS kick tables for I and other rotating pieces.
- Marathon, with a level every 10 lines; 40-line Sprint; two-minute Ultra; Zen with neither gravity nor automatic locking. Sprint and Ultra keep level-1 gravity.
- 500 ms grounded lock delay, at most 15 grounded movement/rotation resets, soft/hard drop scoring, combos, quads, back-to-back awards, all clears and the standalone’s documented three-corner T-spin rule. No mini category is claimed.
- Relaxed / Balanced / Fast input timing, independent of OS repeat. The latest horizontal press wins. Keyboard and multi-pointer touch controls release correctly on cancellation, pause and focus loss.
- A start/resume countdown excluded from active time, five best results per ranked mode, replacement confirmation, explicit finish for Zen, and paused restoration. Incompatible old mixed-form runs reset individually; other game progress and settings remain intact.
- All five synthesized soundtracks: **Classic House — Original** (128 BPM), **Neon Drive** (136), **Midnight Groove** (122), **Arcade Pulse** (144), and **Cello Flow** (104). Classic House retains its original complete 256-step melody, bass, instruments, echoes and compressor settings. The other mixes retain their independent compositions and instruments. Cello Flow retains its Bach-inspired arpeggios and bowed synthesis.
- Independent master/music/effects levels, music/effects switches and an eight-bar preview that works while gameplay is muted. A look-ahead audio-clock scheduler survives stalls without playing missed notes in a burst. Pause cancels scheduled voices; resume preserves position; track changes retire the previous transport.

The new UI uses the collection’s type, panels, buttons and theme variables, with a responsive playfield, lettered beveled pieces, restrained neon glow, smooth movement, clear/drop feedback, fixed-height score messages and integrated settings/help/records. It uses `grid-club-v1`, not the standalone app’s storage keys.

Controls: arrows / A D S move and soft-drop; Up / W / X clockwise; Z anticlockwise; Space hard drop; C / Shift hold; P / Escape pause; R confirms restart. Touch buttons expose the same actions. Modes are selected for the next run without replacing an in-progress board.

## Archive Patrol

An expedition through six named archive sectors, with original ships, environment art, missions and locally synthesized adaptive EDM. The broad exploration/scavenging reference was the developer’s [Operation: Inner Space feature description](https://www.sdispace.com/features.html). Archive Patrol uses a fictional archive and never reads the player’s files.

The main loop is **blast → scoop → upgrade**. Each sector has three close-range combat waves, with a four-second loot break and visible arrival rings. The first targets are within 255 m and the opening cores are already in collection range. Enemy roles include circling skirmishers, three-shot gunships and longer-range snipers. Sectors three and six end with a phased command carrier. Passive wardens no longer clear the action for the player.

Three chassis trade speed, shields and tractor reach. Twinlight, five-pellet Scatter and piercing Lance have unlimited ammunition. Cooldowns prevent weapon-switch exploits and swept collisions stop fast projectiles tunnelling through targets. WASD and arrows share one action map in `src/patrol-input.ts`. All controls are ship-relative: W / Up accelerates along the nose, S / Down brakes without reversing, A D / Left Right turns with angular acceleration. Releasing thrust coasts; lateral damping gradually bends momentum into a turn. Boost builds speed along the ship's heading. Mouse position never changes heading and no target correction exists. Space or click fires forward; R optionally repeats forward fire. Shift boosts, F extends the magnet/scans, and G docks at the workshop. The X and Q keys remain legacy aliases for EMP and boost. A fixed gunsight shows the firing direction. The default camera is 80% zoom (25% more world in each dimension), with a speed readout and wider zoom choices. Legacy saves adopt this view and manual controls once while retaining the expedition.

The independent second weapon is a travelling **directional EMP**, launched with E, right-click or its touch/button control. It sweeps a 60-degree cone to 580 m along the heading at the instant it was fired. A separate capacitor takes 18 active seconds to recharge and has its own meter; firing costs no primary energy and does no direct damage. It clears hostile shots inside the cone and disables raiders for five seconds, carriers for three. Disabled ships coast to rest and cannot steer, fire or ram; normal weapons can still damage them. An electrical ring and countdown show the effect. Recovery re-arms attacks with a visible warning. Pausing freezes the pulse, disable durations and recharge; saved pulses and timers restore paused. Energy remains available for boost.

Raiders drop two glowing cores and guaranteed credit/power-up loot. Supply caches drop three cores and a power-up; volatile caches cause enemy-damaging chain reactions. Ore asteroids also release cores. Each collected core immediately banks 12 credits. Overdrive grants twelve seconds of faster fire and 30% more damage; Magnet grants twenty seconds of wider collection. Kills and pickups extend a six-second score chain up to ×4. Collection, cache explosions and power-ups have their own sounds. Sector five retains clearly warned ion hazards; sector two's surveys are optional.

Clear the waves and meet the core target to finish. Remaining loot sweeps into the ship and the upgrade screen opens automatically; returning home is never required. The workshop remains an optional repair stop, with weapons usable everywhere in flight. Upgrades cost 100/200/300 credits and carry between sectors. Next sector launches straight into the action. Existing ferry-era cargo is banked once at its former promised value, while coordinates, credits, ship choices and upgrades survive migration. Checkpoints, pending arrivals and active power-up timers restore paused. A lethal hit during the final sweep remains a loss. **Repair & rejoin** then restores the ship while preserving credits, collected cores and cleared enemies; it does not regenerate already-paid targets. Temporary power-ups and the score chain reset on repair.

Five difficulty settings increase enemy durability, numbers, fire speed and damage. `tests/patrol-action-balance.ts` flies complete campaigns using ordinary movement, shooting, tractor, pulse and affordable upgrades. The manual-steering balance report covers five levels and three seeds, including repair/rejoin counts and incoming damage. The former assisted-aim report is historical; completion times and damage are not directly comparable. These scripted pilots verify progression and reachable completion, rather than predict every player's experience.

The renderer includes shaded ships, engine trails, parallax stars, nebulae, a crescent planet, a detailed workshop, ore asteroids, bright crystal cores, labelled supply/blast caches, distinct pickups, charge/warp warnings, phased explosions and hit feedback. The cockpit keeps wave count, enemy count, core target, multiplier, credits and timed power-ups visible without moving the board. All five shared themes and reduced-motion preferences are respected. Phone controls include a virtual stick and multi-touch action pads.

`tests/patrol-action-playground.html` on isolated port **5193** exercises ship-relative keyboard handling, mouse independence, directional EMP controls, banked pickups, caches/power-ups, pause, upgrades and 100 theme/viewport/scene layouts. Add `?bundle` to test the offline deliverable. Its fixtures are separate from the user's saved expedition. `tests/patrol-flight.test.ts` checks manual flight and EMP geometry, timing, saves and disabled-enemy rules. `tests/patrol-action-loop.test.ts` checks the action loop; older physics and legacy-save fixtures remain in `tests/archive-patrol.test.ts`.

## Validation

- `npm test --workspace @single-file-games/grid-club`: collection regressions, the ported TetriGrid gameplay/storage/audio tests, integration saves and handling tests, and Archive Patrol mechanics.
- `npm run build --workspace @single-file-games/grid-club`: strict TypeScript and a single-file offline build.
- `/tests/tetrigrid-playground.html`: 150 theme/viewport/state combinations, hover geometry, all four modes, holds, scoring, records, restart cancellation, save restoration and touch cancellation. Add `?bundle` for the offline build.
- `/tests/tetrigrid-audio.html`: complete native Web Audio renders of all five soundtracks, including a sample-by-sample Classic House comparison against the preserved original audio fixture.
- `/tests/patrol-playground.html`: 100 layouts, real mission/combat/docking/checkpoint flows, touch controls and adaptive audio checks. Add `?bundle` for the offline build.
- `node tests/patrol-balance.ts --write`: 45 reproducible campaign balance runs, stored in `tests/results/patrol-balance.json`.
- `tests/results/tetrigrid-source-hashes.json`: read-only baseline hashes for the standalone folder, excluding dependencies, used to verify it was not modified.

Test fixtures are development-only and excluded from the final HTML. The older hint/opponent audit reports retain their original 48-game scope; these two games are real-time solo arcade games.
