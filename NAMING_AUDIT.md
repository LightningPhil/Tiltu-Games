# Game and theme naming review

Research date: **18 October 2026**. Scope: the 48 catalogue games, their named variants, the five themes, and the Grid Club umbrella name. This is a source-backed naming risk review, **not a trademark clearance opinion or a guarantee that a name is available in every country**.

## Outcome

Most catalogue labels describe traditional games, sports or puzzle types. Retain those descriptive labels. The following changes reduce avoidable association with a particular publisher or unresolved claim without changing the games:

| Previous visible label | Chosen visible label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Neon Tron | **Neon Grid** | TRON is an established Disney entertainment property. A descriptive theme label preserves the neon appearance without borrowing that franchise name. |
| LifeGenesis | **Two-Colour Colonies** | LifeGenesis is the title of a particular historical Windows game, rather than the name of Conway's automaton. Use a description of this app's colony mode. |
| TetraVex | **Edge Matching** | A precautionary descriptive alternative to a distinctive title used by other software. This review did **not** establish a currently enforceable TetraVex registration or infringement. |
| Nonograms | **Picture Logic** | There is documented UK trademark/passing-off history for NONOGRAM and a continuing claimant's notice. Current official register status could not be verified. This is a cautious alternative, not a finding that the familiar puzzle-type word is unlawful. |
| Memory Pairs | **Matching Pairs** | Ravensburger expressly brands its matching game memory®. This avoids an unnecessary branding ambiguity; it is not a conclusion that every descriptive use of the word memory infringes. |

Internal game/theme identifiers can stay unchanged so existing saves remain compatible. Historical discussion and source references may identify the original titles factually; they should not become the title of this app's implementation or imply endorsement.

**Grid Club itself remains unresolved.** Grid Learning already uses **GridClub** for a UK-facing educational service containing games. Its own [terms](https://gridclub.com/terms-and-conditions) identify the provider and a licence service, and its [user guide](https://gridclub.com/subscribers/grown-ups/pdfs/User%20Guide%20to%20GridClub.pdf) describes selecting games. The spacing difference does not dispose of possible confusion. This is a more relevant finding than an unrelated business using the same words. Keep the local project name for now, but do not describe it as cleared for public release. A new umbrella brand, or permission where appropriate, needs an actual clearance assessment before launch. No replacement umbrella name is certified by this review.

## Copyright and trademark are different questions

The [U.S. Copyright Office's FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html) says names and titles are not protected by copyright, while artwork and creative expression may be. It also separates ideas/systems from their written or drawn expression. That supports using independently implemented traditional game concepts; it does not grant permission to copy another version's code, sprites, music, diagrams or rulebook wording.

In the UK, the [IPO overview](https://www.gov.uk/intellectual-property-an-overview/what-ip-is) distinguishes copyright from branding rights. Its [registration guidance](https://www.gov.uk/how-to-register-a-trade-mark/what-you-can-register) explains why descriptive/common terms face registration objections and why similar marks for related goods/services matter. Descriptive use is not the same thing as owning the name as a brand. Neither this principle nor the absence of a search result establishes that all existing rights have disappeared.

The research is UK-focused, with US guidance and specific Japanese/Canadian evidence where useful. Rights depend on territory, goods/services, actual presentation and earlier use. The [USPTO's clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks) also calls for checking confusingly similar marks and common-law commercial use. An exact-word search alone is insufficient.

## Evidence and access limits

- Inspected catalogue labels in `src/catalog.ts`, `src/club-plus/meta.ts`, theme labels in `src/main.ts`, and Life/Poker/Patience mode labels. The matrix below covers every catalogue game; the next table covers the named variants and themes.
- Researched the names through current web searches, primary publisher/developer pages, federation rules and museum collections. Traditional use of a name supports the descriptive-label recommendation; a rule page is **not** a legal clearance certificate.
- Followed the [UKIPO official search link](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark) to its keyword service. The research reader received HTTP 403. The [USPTO live search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) returned no readable results. Therefore this report does **not** claim completed live-register searches for all 48 names, negative search certificates, or verified absence of pending/similar marks.
- Official indexed decisions and accessible Canadian records are cited as those particular records, with their territory and limits. Historical decisions are not treated as proof of current status. The [WIPO Global Brand Database guidance](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database) itself recommends national/regional register checks as well.
- No conclusion is drawn from other apps merely using a name. Widespread unauthorised use can coexist with rights. Conversely, an old trademark notice does not prove a registration remains enforceable today.

## Specific rights findings that must not be flattened into “all generic”

1. **Grid Club / GridClub:** relevant existing UK-facing education-and-games use. Current registration and passing-off assessment remain outstanding. Consider a different umbrella name before public distribution; changing a space is not a clearance strategy.
2. **Sudoku, Kakuro, Slitherlink:** Nikoli publishes concrete Japanese registration claims. This is a territorial concern, not proof of infringement by the UK descriptive labels. The English Sudoku page explicitly identifies Japan. A globally accessible release needs a territory-aware decision.
3. **NONOGRAM:** the 2000 UKIPO opposition is historical evidence, not proof of a current registration. The claimant's site remains available; third-party “dead” status is not accepted here as official verification. The chosen Picture Logic label avoids depending on that unresolved question.
4. **TetraVex:** no enforceable current registration was established. Renaming to Edge Matching is optional risk reduction adopted for this app, not an accusation against GNOME or other implementers.
5. **TRON, Othello, Connect 4, The Game of Life, memory®, Picross:** documented publisher/franchise branding. Use the chosen descriptive labels instead. Nintendo's [own page](https://www.nintendo.com/kr/guide/software/puzzle.html) calls Picross its trademark; the [US TTAB decision](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-77245239-EXA-27.pdf) found acquired distinctiveness for PICROSS in the application at issue. Do not infer that a descriptive origin defeats trademark protection.

The new descriptive labels are proposed as game/mode descriptions inside the collection. They are not certified as unique, registrable replacement brands. This report reviews names, not the provenance of every image, sound, font, puzzle or dependency. [Microsoft's copyright permissions page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/copyright/permissions) separately makes clear that its software/content and third-party content require the applicable permission; historical inspiration is not an asset licence.

## Release decision

The app can keep the traditional/descriptive labels identified above, with original content and factual rules attribution. **There is no responsible basis to say that every current name is “certainly cleared” worldwide.** The remaining material launch question is the **Grid Club** umbrella brand, plus jurisdiction-specific treatment of the Nikoli names if Japan or a broad international market is intended. Use an IP professional's live-register and prior-use search to resolve those particular questions before a public commercial launch.
